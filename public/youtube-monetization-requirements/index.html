YouTube Monetization Requirements 2026

YouTube has two practical Partner Program milestones: earlier access to fan-funding features at 500 subscribers in eligible regions, and ad revenue sharing at 1,000 subscribers. This guide explains the numbers, the rules behind them, and what Shorts and long-form creators should track.

Quick answer

For full advertising revenue sharing, a channel generally needs 1,000 subscribers and either 4,000 valid public watch hours from long-form videos in the previous 12 months or 10 million valid public Shorts views in the previous 90 days. Reaching a number does not guarantee approval: YouTube reviews the entire channel and applies additional account, location, and policy requirements.

YouTube monetization requirements at a glance

The lower and higher thresholds unlock different benefits. The 500-subscriber milestone is not a shortcut to Watch Page ad revenue. It provides earlier access to selected fan-funding and Shopping features where the expanded YPP is available. The 1,000-subscriber milestone is the route to revenue sharing from Watch Page ads, Shorts Feed ads, and YouTube Premium.

Tier 1: the 500-subscriber requirements for beginners

The expanded YPP gives qualifying creators earlier access to monetization features without yet unlocking advertising revenue sharing. A creator needs 500 subscribers, three valid public uploads during the previous 90 days, and one of two performance routes: 3,000 valid public watch hours during the previous 12 months or 3 million valid public Shorts views during the previous 90 days.

This tier is useful for beginners because it can unlock features such as channel memberships, Super Chat, Super Stickers, Super Thanks, and selected Shopping options, subject to each feature’s separate eligibility rules. Availability depends on the creator’s country or region. A channel based outside an expanded-YPP location cannot use the lower threshold simply because its audience is located in an eligible country.

The upload condition matters. A channel with an old viral library may have enough subscribers and watch time but still need three qualifying public uploads in the latest 90-day period. Private, unlisted, deleted, or otherwise ineligible uploads should not be treated as a reliable way to satisfy this requirement.

Tier 2: requirements for ads and YouTube Premium revenue

The higher tier is what most creators mean when they ask about YouTube monetization requirements. A channel needs 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 valid public watch hours from long-form public videos in the last 12 months or 10 million valid public Shorts views in the last 90 days.

After approval and acceptance of the relevant monetization modules, this tier can provide revenue sharing from Watch Page ads, Shorts Feed ads, and YouTube Premium. It can also include the fan-funding features available at the earlier tier when the channel and individual features remain eligible.

The thresholds make a channel eligible to apply; they do not create automatic approval. YouTube reviews the channel as a whole. Reviewers can consider its main theme, most-viewed and newest videos, the content generating the largest share of watch time, metadata, and other signals when applying channel monetization policies.

YouTube monetization requirements for Shorts

Shorts creators use the same subscriber milestones but have a separate view-based performance route. Earlier YPP access requires 3 million valid public Shorts views within 90 days; full ad-revenue eligibility requires 10 million within 90 days. The window is rolling, so older views fall out as new days enter the period.

Watch time generated inside the Shorts Feed does not count toward the 3,000- or 4,000-hour long-form routes. A creator who publishes both formats should monitor the two paths separately in YouTube Studio rather than adding Shorts Feed watch time to long-form watch hours.

Shorts revenue works differently from Watch Page advertising. Ads appear between videos in the Shorts Feed, eligible revenue is pooled, music licensing is accounted for, and monetizing creators keep 45% of their allocated amount after accepting the Shorts Monetization Module. The threshold determines whether a creator can apply; it does not predict RPM or monthly payment.

Views must be eligible for revenue sharing. Artificial views, ineligible Shorts, and views accumulated before accepting applicable monetization terms do not create a guaranteed payment. See YouTube’s official Shorts monetization policies for current revenue-pool rules.

YouTube monetization requirements for long videos

Long-form creators should focus on valid public watch hours, not total channel watch time shown in every analytics view. YouTube measures the relevant hours over the most recent 12 months. Watch time from private, unlisted, or deleted videos does not qualify. Watch time from ad campaigns and Shorts viewed in the Shorts Feed also does not count toward the long-form threshold.

Live streams can contribute when they are public and converted to public video-on-demand content, but unlisted, deleted, or streams that never become VOD do not count. Because the 12-month window rolls forward daily, a channel can gain hours and still see the eligibility total fall when a strong month from the previous year expires.

There is no official requirement for a certain average view duration, video length, upload frequency at the higher tier, or number of views per video. Those factors can affect how quickly a creator accumulates watch hours, but they are not substitutes for the stated subscriber, watch-hour, Shorts-view, account, and policy conditions.

YPP eligibility checklist before you apply

Numbers are only one part of the application. Before selecting Apply Now in YouTube Studio, verify every item below:

  • Your channel follows YouTube channel monetization policies.
  • You live in a country or region where the relevant YPP tier is available.
  • Your channel has no active Community Guidelines strikes.
  • Two-step verification is enabled for the Google Account.
  • The channel has access to YouTube’s advanced features.
  • An active AdSense for YouTube account is linked, or you are ready to create one through YouTube Studio.
  • The subscriber and valid watch-hour or Shorts-view threshold appears as completed in the Earn tab.

Create a new AdSense for YouTube account only through YouTube Studio when the application asks for it. Duplicate or incorrectly created AdSense accounts can delay the process. Once the base terms are accepted and an active AdSense account is associated, the channel enters the review queue.

How to check YouTube monetization requirements

Channel owners should use YouTube Studio → Earn for authoritative eligibility counters and application status. Public channel pages do not reveal valid public watch hours, valid Shorts views for YPP, policy standing, linked AdSense status, or whether every required monetization module has been accepted.

If you are researching another public channel, you can check if a channel is monetized based on public signals. YPPCheck compares public subscribers and modeled activity with the two numeric tiers, but its result is an estimate rather than proof of enrollment. Once you have a view forecast, use the YouTube earnings calculator to model a revenue range by RPM.

Common YouTube monetization misunderstandings

Ads do not prove the creator is being paid

YouTube may place ads on content from channels that are not receiving a revenue share. A copyright owner can also monetize a claimed video. Seeing an advertisement is therefore not a dependable public test of YPP enrollment.

The yellow dollar sign is not necessarily channel demonetization

A yellow monetization icon in YouTube Studio usually means a particular video has limited or no ads under advertiser-friendly content guidelines. The channel may remain in YPP while that video receives lower ad demand or revenue.

A Content ID claim is different from a strike or YPP removal

A Content ID claim can allow a rights holder to monetize, track, or block a specific upload. A copyright strike is a separate enforcement action. Channel demonetization is also separate and can occur for channel-level monetization-policy problems.

Four thousand hours do not guarantee a payment amount

Watch hours are an eligibility signal, not a payment unit. Revenue depends on monetized playbacks, geography, topic, season, format, advertiser demand, YouTube Premium viewing, ad suitability, and other factors. YouTube does not promise a fixed amount per watch hour.

A channel can lose monetization later

YouTube continues checking channels after acceptance. Policy violations, invalid traffic, reused or repetitious content concerns, account issues, and other compliance problems can remove monetization. YouTube also reserves the right to turn off monetization on channels that have not uploaded a video or posted to the Posts tab for six months or more.

YouTube monetization requirements FAQ

Can I monetize YouTube with 500 subscribers?

In eligible expanded-YPP regions, 500 subscribers can unlock an application for selected fan-funding and Shopping features when the channel also has three valid public uploads in 90 days and either 3,000 valid public watch hours in 12 months or 3 million valid public Shorts views in 90 days. It does not by itself unlock ad revenue sharing.

Do Shorts watch hours count toward 4,000 hours?

No. Public watch hours generated by views in the Shorts Feed do not count toward the 4,000-hour route. Shorts use the separate 10-million-valid-public-views route for full YPP eligibility.

Are 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 hours enough?

They qualify a channel to apply through the long-form route, but approval is not automatic. The channel must meet YouTube’s account, location, policy, strike, advanced-feature, verification, and AdSense requirements and pass channel review.

How long does YouTube monetization review take?

Review time varies. YouTube places completed applications into a review queue and may need additional time when applications are unusually numerous, multiple reviews are required, or systems are delayed. The Earn tab is the authoritative place to monitor status.

Can public tools confirm YPP membership?

No. YouTube does not expose a public API field for YPP enrollment. Public tools can compare visible statistics with thresholds, but only the channel owner’s YouTube Studio can confirm current application and monetization status.
