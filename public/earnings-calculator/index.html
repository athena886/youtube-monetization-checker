Understand the model

How YouTube revenue estimates work

The simplest useful estimate multiplies monthly views by RPM, then divides by 1,000. If a channel receives 100,000 views at a $4 RPM, its modeled platform revenue is $400.

Real results vary widely. Audience geography, viewer age, content category, video length, ad suitability, season, and advertiser demand can move RPM in either direction. A view does not guarantee an ad impression, and not every impression pays the same amount.

RPM vs CPM

Use the creator-side number

CPM describes what advertisers pay per 1,000 ad impressions before YouTube’s revenue share. RPM reflects what the creator earns per 1,000 total views after revenue share and can include multiple YouTube revenue sources.

For planning, RPM is usually the more practical input. Use your channel’s historical RPM from YouTube Studio when available. If you do not have one, choose the closest niche preset and treat the result as a scenario—not a promise.

Revenue questions

YouTube earnings calculator FAQ

Use these answers to interpret the range instead of treating a single estimate as guaranteed income.

How much does YouTube pay per 1,000 views?

There is no universal rate. Creator revenue per 1,000 total views is commonly measured as RPM and varies with audience geography, niche, format, season, ad suitability, and advertiser demand.

What is YouTube RPM?

RPM is the creator's estimated revenue per 1,000 total views after YouTube's revenue share. It can include several YouTube revenue sources and is different from advertiser CPM.

Is CPM the same as creator earnings?

No. CPM measures what advertisers pay per 1,000 ad impressions before revenue share. It does not equal the amount a creator receives per 1,000 total video views.

Do YouTube Shorts earn the same RPM as long-form videos?

Usually not. Shorts use a pooled revenue-sharing model and often produce a lower effective RPM than long-form videos, although actual results vary by audience and music usage.

Does this estimate include sponsorships and affiliate income?

No. The calculator models YouTube platform revenue only. Sponsorships, affiliates, products, memberships outside the modeled RPM, taxes, and expenses are not added.

How accurate is this YouTube earnings estimate?

It is a planning scenario, not a revenue prediction. A creator's historical RPM in YouTube Studio is the best available input, while niche presets should be treated as broad starting points.
